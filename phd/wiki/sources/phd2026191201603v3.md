## Skim (pass 1)

**Your recall:**

This paper present a novel agent called Dreamer which is capable of learning long-horizon behaviors by latent imagination. The novelty in this paper is the capacities of the model to learn from imagination before acting on its own.

**Recall check** (Section 3, "Learning Behaviors by Latent Imagination"): What is the actual mechanism by which Dreamer's action/value models are trained in latent imagination—does it "learn from imagination before acting," or does it use analytic gradients of value estimates backpropagated through the learned latent dynamics?

**Your triage verdict:** bank-and-stop

**My own read (after yours):**

My read: Dreamer's core contribution is elegant and clearly stated—rather than choosing between short-horizon imagined-reward maximization or expensive derivative-free planning, it trains an actor-critic entirely inside the learned latent space, backpropagating analytic gradients of a λ-return value estimate through the dynamics model. The horizon ablation (Figure 4) is the most convincing piece of evidence here: it isolates the value model's contribution and shows it's specifically what buys robustness to short imagination horizons, rather than the latent imagination framing alone doing the work. Worth flagging as a limitation—the paper is explicit that world model and behavior learning are decoupled (representation learning is "orthogonal"), and the reconstruction-based world model clearly outperforms contrastive and reward-only variants (Figure 8), meaning a decent chunk of Dreamer's empirical gains may be riding on PlaNet's RSSM/pixel-reconstruction machinery rather than the actor-critic innovation per se. Also notable: the discrete-action/Atari results (Appendix C) are included but explicitly caveated as not yet competitive, which is an honest signal about where latent-imagination methods still lag model-free baselines at the time. Connects directly to SVG, DDPG/SAC, and MVE/STEVE as the "analytic gradient" and "multi-step return" lineages it's synthesizing.
