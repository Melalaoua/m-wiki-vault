# If You Build It, They Will Come

In this article, [[Ben Landau-Taylor]] argues that the fastest and most reliable way to integrate into any social scene or community is to actively organize events around its core activity, transitioning from a passive consumer to an active producer of social fabric.

## Key Themes

### The Consumer Attitude vs. Social Production
Many people view social circles as automatic features of the world—like a wild blueberry bush that naturally sprouts parties, dinners, and reading groups. In reality, these events only happen because someone puts in the logistical legwork. Because most people avoid this effort, those who actually organize events quickly become the leaders of the scene.

### The Asymmetry of Social Demand
There is a massive, structural undersupply of social organization. The demand for social events and gatherings is almost limitless, while the supply of organizers is incredibly low. Consequently, organizing events is an incredibly high-leverage way to build relationships. Other organizers are highly attuned to this labor and will quickly welcome and respect anyone else who helps carry the burden of [[Social Fabric Production]].

### Addressing Social Alienation
Modern social alienation is driven in part by a surplus of free riders who wish to consume social connection without producing it. While systemic solutions are hard, individuals can solve this at their own scale by actively building the community they want to see.

## Key claims

- The fastest way to join a social group is to organize events around its core activity. ([[personal/raw/www.benlandautaylor.com/if-you-build-it-they-will-come#Introduction]]) — "the best and fastest way to join a group is to organize events where we do the group’s core activity"
- There is a severe imbalance in every community where demand for social events far outstrips the supply. ([[personal/raw/www.benlandautaylor.com/if-you-build-it-they-will-come#Introduction]]) — "In every group I’ve ever encountered, there is far more demand for social events and things to do than there is supply."
- Social scenes are not natural phenomena; they only occur because someone actively handles the logistics. ([[personal/raw/www.benlandautaylor.com/if-you-build-it-they-will-come#Introduction]]) — "In fact, events happen when someone puts in the legwork to organize them."
- A community's leadership is primarily comprised of the individuals who actually bother to do the work of organizing. ([[personal/raw/www.benlandautaylor.com/if-you-build-it-they-will-come#Introduction]]) — "A scene’s leaders are mostly the people who actually bother to put in the work."
