# Cognitive Debt

**Cognitive debt** is the accumulated gap between the code/systems one has produced or shipped and one's actual understanding of how they work — analogous to technical debt, but about comprehension rather than code quality.

Coined/used in this sense by [[Ankur Sethi]] in [[Prevent cognitive debt by manually retyping LLM-generated code]], describing what happens when developers let LLM coding assistants "roam free" over a codebase: features get one-shotted, PRs get auto-generated and rubber-stamped, and the human loses the mental model of their own system.

Sethi's antidote: force manual re-entry of all LLM-proposed code so understanding is built alongside output, trading speed (his estimate: ~2x vs ~10x) for comprehension. He frames unchecked cognitive debt as an industry-wide risk — a point where "we no longer understand how large parts of our digital infrastructure are put together."

Related to [[Cognitive Attention Degradation]] (attention/understanding erosion from passive consumption) and contrasts with [[Deliberate Practice in Gaming]]'s insistence on active, effortful engagement over passive execution.
